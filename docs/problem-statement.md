# Problem Statement

## Background

Ports are critical parts of global supply chains and handle large numbers of
vessels, containers, berths, cranes, and yard operations. The L1 industry
problem highlights that port operators allocate berths, cranes, and yard space
across hundreds of vessels, often using manual spreadsheet-based processes.

## The Problem

When many vessels arrive at the same time, port resources can become
overloaded and congestion develops. The problem is that congestion hotspots
may only be recognized after vessels are already waiting offshore.

This makes it difficult for port operators to respond early, choose alternate
routes, and efficiently allocate limited berths and cranes.

CongestiQ aims to predict upcoming congestion before it becomes severe and
help operators decide how to respond.

## Who Is Affected?

The problem affects:

- Port operators
- Port operations managers
- Shift supervisors
- Shipping companies
- Vessel operators
- Logistics and supply-chain businesses
- Cargo owners

## Why It Matters

Port congestion can cause vessels to wait for long periods and can create
delays across the wider supply chain.

The industry problem statement uses the 2021 Los Angeles/Long Beach port
backlog as an example, where more than 100 ships were waiting offshore for
weeks and the disruption cost global supply chains more than $10 billion.

Early identification of congestion can give port operators more time to
allocate resources and make operational decisions.

## Why Existing Solutions Fall Short

Current port operations can rely heavily on manual planning using
spreadsheets. This makes it difficult to continuously analyze vessel
schedules, berth capacity, and upcoming congestion across hundreds of
vessels.

Congestion is often identified reactively, after vessels are already
queuing. By that point, alternate routing and resource-allocation decisions
may come too late to prevent significant delays.

CongestiQ addresses this gap by providing proactive AI-assisted decision
support for congestion prediction, alternate routing, berth and crane
allocation, and a prioritized 72-hour port operations plan.
